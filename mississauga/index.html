What’s possible in Mississauga

Across Mississauga, provincial and city rules now let many single-family lots add homes that weren’t allowed a few years ago — commonly three or four, depending on the lot and the neighbourhood. Larger suburban lots often have the room; we check whether yours does.

Two ways to add homes

Convert what’s there

Keep your existing house and add or divide units. Usually faster, less expensive and less disruptive — a good fit when the house is sound and the lot has room.

Build new

Tear down and build a purpose-built multiplex. More homes, a modern building, and the owner’s suite designed as a real home — but a bigger budget and a longer timeline.

What to consider before you start

  • Your lot’s size, shape and frontage — it sets how many homes are realistic.
  • Whether you’ll live in one unit or hold the whole building.
  • Your budget and timeline — converting and building new are very different.
  • The street and your neighbours — a building that fits the area gets approved faster.
  • The numbers — what it rents for has to support what it costs to build.
