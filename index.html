The Idea

They call it “house-hacking.” We grew up calling it home.

The fear

The idea is old and simple: own a small multi-unit building, live in the best part of it, and let the other homes help carry the mortgage. Across Ontario it gets branded “house-hacking” — a clever trick, trailed by every nightmare-landlord story you’ve heard. That word is what scares good people away from a genuinely good home.

The reframe

In Montreal, we never used it. For generations it was simply how families lived — the owner’s side the largest and the nicest, with the yard, the garage and the bedrooms together, and steady neighbours in the homes alongside. Done properly, it isn’t a hack. It’s live-in ownership: a real home that quietly pays for itself.

The craft

The building was never the hard part. The craft is living well alongside your tenants — the courtesy, the boundaries, the small rules that keep everyone comfortable. That is what we grew up with, and the part most owners here were simply never taught.

Common Questions

Multiplexes in Ontario, in plain language.

New rules, new options. Here are the basics — then we check your address and explore what’s realistic, free.

What is a multiplex?
A multiplex is simply a house with more than one home inside it — a triplex (three homes), a fourplex (four), up to a sixplex (six) — often with a garden suite in the backyard. You can live in one unit and let the others help carry the mortgage, or hold the whole building as an investment.
Can I turn my detached house into a fourplex in Ontario?
Often, yes. Ontario’s recent rules make it much easier to add homes on lots that used to allow only one. Whether yours qualifies depends on the lot and the city — and that’s the first thing we check, free, when you send us your address.
Should I tear down or convert my existing house?
There are two paths. Converting keeps your existing house and adds or carves out units — usually faster and less expensive. Tearing down and building new gives you more homes and a modern building, but costs more and takes longer. The right call depends on your house, your lot and your budget — we walk you through both, honestly.
How many homes can my lot hold?
It depends on the lot’s size and shape and your city’s rules — commonly three or four homes, sometimes up to six where permitted, plus a garden suite. We look at your specific address and tell you what’s realistic before you spend anything.
