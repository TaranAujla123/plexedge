The Idea

They call it “house-hacking.” We grew up calling it a home.

The fear

The idea is old and simple: own a small multi-unit building, live in the best part of it, and let the other homes help carry the mortgage. Across Ontario it gets branded “house-hacking” — a clever trick, trailed by every nightmare-landlord story you’ve heard. That word is what scares good people away from a genuinely good home.

The reframe

In Montreal, we never used it. For generations it was simply how families lived — the owner’s side the largest and the nicest, with the yard, the garage and the bedrooms together, and steady neighbours in the homes alongside. Done properly, it isn’t a hack. It’s live-in ownership: a real home that quietly pays for itself.

The craft

The building was never the hard part. The craft is living well alongside your tenants — the courtesy, the boundaries, the small rules that keep everyone comfortable. That is what we grew up with, and the part most owners here were simply never taught.

Financing

The financing is part of the advantage.

Multiplexes have their own lending playbook — and used right, it works in your favour. We bring in a mortgage-broker partner, family, who lives in these CMHC programs and works with multiple lenders.

Live in 1–4 units

CMHC-insured, low down payment

Owner-occupied, CMHC lets you buy a duplex with as little as 5% down, or a triplex or fourplex you live in with 10% down — turning a bigger building into a reachable purchase.

Add a suite

Refinance to build a unit

CMHC’s refinance program can help fund a secondary suite — basement, in-law or garden — on your existing home, up to four units and up to a 30-year amortization.

5+ units

CMHC MLI Select

For five units and up, MLI Select rewards buildings that are affordable, energy-efficient or accessible with better terms — higher leverage, longer amortization and lower premiums.

Because we keep the income units at attainable rents, projects often line up with exactly what these programs reward. Your broker structures the specifics; we make sure the building qualifies. One more thing you don’t figure out alone.

Honest answers

Common worries, answered.

The real questions owners are quietly weighing — answered straight.

“I don’t want other people living right next to me.”

Fair — but it’s worth being honest about the trade. A multiplex is designed and built for privacy: proper sound separation, smart layouts and real construction — no different than a condo, a townhouse, or a new detached home built a few feet from the next. Day to day, they’re just neighbours. So the real question is this: would you rather have no one next door and stretch to afford where you live — or have neighbours and barely notice your monthly cost? You own the building, you live in the biggest unit — the one that actually feels like home — and the other homes carry most of the bill.

“Do I have to be a landlord?”

Only as much as you want to be. You run your own building, and we help you run it well — including finding and screening the right tenants through PlexRent.ca (in development). It’s a home first.

“What if the numbers don’t work?”

“Isn’t this risky?”

“Can I sell it later?”

Common Questions

Multiplexes in Ontario, in plain language.

New rules, new options. Here are the basics — then we check your address and explore what’s realistic, free.

What is a multiplex?
A multiplex is simply a house with more than one home inside it — a triplex (three homes), a fourplex (four), up to a sixplex (six) — often with a garden suite in the backyard. You can live in one unit and let the others help carry the mortgage, or hold the whole building as an investment.
Can I turn my detached house into a fourplex in Ontario?
Often, yes. Ontario’s recent rules make it much easier to add homes on lots that used to allow only one. Whether yours qualifies depends on the lot and the city — and that’s the first thing we check, free, when you send us your address.
Should I tear down or convert my existing house?
There are two paths. Converting keeps your existing house and adds or carves out units — usually faster and less expensive. Tearing down and building new gives you more homes and a modern building, but costs more and takes longer. The right call depends on your house, your lot and your budget — we walk you through both, honestly.
How many homes can my lot hold?
It depends on the lot’s size and shape and your city’s rules — commonly three or four homes, sometimes up to six where permitted, plus a garden suite. We look at your specific address and tell you what’s realistic before you spend anything.
Where does PlexEdge work?
Across Ontario, with a focus on Toronto, Hamilton, Kitchener-Waterloo and the Greater Toronto Area. If you’re elsewhere in the province, still reach out — we’ll tell you honestly whether we can help.
